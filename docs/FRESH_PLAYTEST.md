# Optional fresh-player check

Use the local preview on a new run. Let the player work without route coaching. Leave sound on for the main run; a short muted check can separately verify that the written instructions work. Do not show encounter screenshots beforehand.

Record where and when the player gets lost, their unprompted comments, their first visible reaction to Sakiko, uncertainty about an interaction, and each capture and its apparent cause. Record completion time without treating a target duration as a pass/fail rule.

After play, ask:

- What were you trying to do at each discovery?
- Where did you expect to go next?
- What stood out about Sakiko's face?
- Why do you think you got caught?
- What do you think happened to Sakiko?
- Which moment stayed with you?

Check whether the player can explain the next task, identify required objects without repeated long searches, explain captures through readable mistakes, and understand the human story without optional records. Note whether the distant hallway appearance still works. Avoid suggesting an answer or asking whether something is “scarier now.”

The implementer's route tests establish functionality. They do not establish fear, audio quality, or comprehension for a new player.

For the middle section, observe whether the player recognizes the hand mechanism, chooses to interrupt it on hearing an approach, uses a connected classroom route, and can explain a capture. Record the section's real start/end time. Do not disclose operation duration, hearing radius, enemy position, or a preferred escape route while measuring these reactions.

At dawn, a student came through the school gates carrying four keys and a small photograph.

The photograph showed Sakiko at a desk, scissors in her hand. Someone had called her name. She had looked up before there was time to arrange her face.

No painted mouth. No smile.

The school had kept a different account of her.

An accident during festival preparations. A student in a restricted room. A notice asking families to respect the privacy of those involved.

There was no mention of the door being locked from outside.

The belongings left behind named four classmates. Their notes disagreed about who had carried the key, who had promised to return, who had been the last to leave.

They agreed on one thing: Sakiko was supposed to come out before morning.

Her letter was still folded in her bag.

She had asked to help in the library the following year. Somewhere quiet, where nobody would have to look at her.

One of the former classmates agreed to meet the student near the station.

She kept her coat on. When the festival was mentioned, she said they had meant to frighten Sakiko. They had expected her to shout, or cry, or bang on the door until someone let her out.

She could remember the laughter in the corridor.

She could not remember when it stopped.

“It wasn't only the four of us,” she said.

Then she asked about the enlarged photograph. Whether it was still hanging. Whether the painted face had survived.

She asked for it to be destroyed.

The small photograph went to Sakiko's mother instead.

She recognized the scissors before she recognized the room.

“She used those to cut her fringe,” she said. “I always told her it came out crooked.”

She brought the picture nearer to the window.

For a moment, she was looking at a daughter who still had an afternoon ahead of her.

Then she asked whether the photograph could stay.

The school gates remained shut.

The photograph was placed beside Sakiko's undelivered letter. Her mother had it copied so the original would not fade in the light.

There was no photograph of the library she might have worked in. No letter from the following year.

Only a request that had never reached anyone.

The maintenance report was not brought to the house.

At 08:40 on the morning of 16 October, the caretaker had opened the former rehearsal room. Sakiko was found shortly afterward. By evening, she had been taken away.

At 18:10, the same caretaker returned to secure the room.

Beneath the morning's entry, in the same handwriting, he recorded knocking inside.

He underlined “inside” twice.

He left no record of opening the door again.

You found a photograph of Sakiko without the smile.

It had been folded behind the larger one, protected from the damp. She was sitting at a desk with a pair of scissors in her hand. Someone had called her name while she was working. She had looked up.

That was all.

You kept returning to that photograph afterward.

The other face was easier to remember. The mouth stretching open. The hair filling the corridor. Sometimes, when you closed your eyes, you could still judge how far away she was by the sound of it dragging across the floor.

But this was the face she had lived with.

The school had kept very little.

A notice. A maintenance report. A letter to parents explaining that a student had entered a restricted room during festival preparations.

The word ‘locked’ did not appear.

Neither did the names of the four students whose belongings you had found.

One of them eventually agreed to speak to you. She chose a café near the station and kept her coat on.

She said they had only meant to leave Sakiko there until everyone went home.

When you asked about the photograph, she looked toward the window.

‘Everybody laughed,’ she said.

You waited.

‘It wasn’t only us.’

She could not remember who had the key last. She remembered asking. She remembered someone saying it had been taken care of.

Before she left, she asked whether you had found the cardboard head.

You told her you had.

She asked you to destroy it.

You took the small photograph to Sakiko’s mother instead.

She recognized the scissors before she recognized the room.

‘She used those for everything,’ she said. ‘Even cutting her fringe.’

She held the picture close to the window. For a moment, you thought she might smile.

Then she asked whether there were any others.

You said no.

The school gates have stayed shut since that night. You still pass them on your way to the station. Nothing moves behind the classroom windows.

You would like to believe she has stopped looking.

There is one thing you have not given her mother.

The maintenance report says the storeroom was opened at eight forty in the morning. Sakiko was found shortly afterward.

There is a second entry beneath it, written by the same caretaker.

At six ten that evening, he returned to secure the room.

He recorded that someone was knocking inside.

He underlined ‘inside’ twice.
